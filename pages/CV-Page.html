Profile

I am an instructional leader and head of department who grows people and improves systems at the same time. I lead an eight-teacher science department across grades 6 to 12 with two EAL specialists, supervise staff through a structured annual growth cycle with formal appraisal, and manage a zero-based budget of around US$50,000. Across four countries I have built the data and assessment systems a school runs on, served on accreditation visiting teams, sat on hiring panels from lab technician to Head of School, and designed wellbeing routines that lower stress while improving support. I build capacity before tools. Wellbeing is at the heart of how I lead: caring for students and staff is what lets a community take on the harder questions about where education is going.

Chemistry and Physics combined, both levels, before and after the program was introduced. Biology is excluded because the earlier year's data was not available to me. Figures count IA entries rather than students, since some candidates sit both subjects, and each year is a different cohort.

The top band did not move, and I would not expect it to. A seven is scrutinized closely by moderators because it indicates near-perfection, and students who reach one were generally always going to. The change sits in the lower half: the grade four band halved and nothing fell below it. That is where the workshop was aimed. When students understand what the task is actually asking of them, the internal assessment becomes approachable, and that shows up here first.
